Who it is for

We invite faculty in every school, including teaching faculty. The focus is research, scholarship and service.

Watching recordings alone counts as full participation.

How the tiers work

There are three tiers. Each has its own page.

  • Entry: use AI well in chat.
  • Mid: give it a folder (Claude Code).
  • Advanced: build systems others can run.

There are three ways to learn each tier. Anyone can move between them.

  • Lecture demos: large sessions on Zoom, recorded.
  • Small groups: 5–10 people with a faculty facilitator.
  • Self-service: any time, using this site.

Where to start

Four ways in. Pick the one that sounds most like you.

You areStart here
Curious or skepticalGo to an AI Exchange, then the Entry tier.
Chat user (uses Claude or HopGPT in a browser)Join an Entry small group, then Mid.
Coder (writes Python or R, has not used agents)Go straight to Mid.
Builder (already uses Claude Code)Go to Advanced.

The time it takes

Each topic runs as a two-week cycle.

  1. A demo is given and recorded.
  2. The recording, a handout and a paired LinkedIn Learning course go up the same day.
  3. Small groups meet the following week.
  4. Finished work is shown at an AI Exchange or the spring showcase.

Self-service goes at your pace. LinkedIn Learning lists each course's length.

Department AI representatives

We are asking each department for one faculty AI representative, named by the chair. They pass news into the department and bring its needs back.

Ask your chair who yours is. Departments are naming them this winter.
